Born-Again Tree — Preflop Raiser vs Checker

Situation: You are the preflop raiser. Villain checks to you on the flop.
Actions: CHECK | BET 1/3 pot | BET 2/3 pot
Baseline avg: Check 38% | Bet 1/3 31% | Bet 2/3 31%
Key concept: Range advantage = who has the stronger overall range on this board
Step 1: Check SPR
SPR ≤ 5?
  YES → SHALLOW GAME (Step 2A)
  NO  → DEEP GAME (Step 2B)
SPR is the root of everything
Shallow = fight on range strength. Deep = fight on position.
Step 2A: Shallow Game (SPR ≤ 5)
Key Question: "Do I have range advantage?"
High cards favor the raiser. Low connected boards favor the caller.

3 Universal Principles
Principle 1: Bet Size Follows Depth
Shallow = big bets (2/3 pot). Deep = small bets (1/3 pot).
Shallow needs bigger bets to matter. Deep uses small bets to build pots over streets.
Principle 2: Board Texture Only Matters in ONE Spot
Low SPR + range disadvantage. That's it.
In all other spots, range composition and position drive decisions, not the board.
Principle 3: Air is the Brake Pedal
More air in your range = more checking. Always. Every corner of the tree.
Air increases check frequency universally.

Raiser Advantage Boards
Boards Where Raiser Has Range Advantage
Ace-high boards — Raiser has all the Ax premiums (AA, AK, AQ, AJ)
King-high boards — Raiser dominates (KK, KQ, KJ, AK)
Queen-high boards — Raiser still ahead (QQ, AQ, KQ)
Broadway-heavy — AKJ, KQT, AQJ etc.
High paired boards — KKx, QQx, JJx (raiser has these pairs more)
Key insight: The higher the board cards, the bigger your range advantage as the raiser.
Boards Where Caller Has Range Advantage
Low connected boards — 654, 765, 543 (caller has 67s, 45s, 56s)
Middle connected boards — 987, T98 (caller has suited connectors)
Low paired boards — 55x, 44x, 33x (caller has these pocket pairs)
Monotone low boards — Caller has more suited combos in these ranks
Key insight: The lower and more connected the board, the more the caller's range catches up or overtakes.
